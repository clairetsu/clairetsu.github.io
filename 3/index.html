03   Fun with Flow Models

CS180: Intro to Computer Vision and Computational Photography

Part A 1.1: Constructing Noisy Training Inputs

interpolate computes xt = (1 − t)ε + t x1, so t = 0 is pure noise and t = 1 is the clean image. I used a photo of my cat Oreo and one noise sample for all three times.

Part A 1.2: Classical Denoising

Each input is divided by t to restore the image's amplitude, then Gaussian blurred. I tried σ = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 and kept the one with the lowest MSE to the original. Noisier inputs needed more blur (σ = 8 at t = 0.25), which also erased most of the detail.

Part A 1.3: One-Step Denoising

one_step_denoise jumps straight to the clean estimate with one velocity prediction: x̂1 = xt + (1 − t) vθ(xt, t, c), using the prompt "a high quality photo". The estimate is much cleaner than the blur, but at t = 0.25 it is smooth and loses the fur texture.

Part A 1.4: Visualizing Velocity

The predicted update is (1 − t)v̂t and the ground truth update is x1 − xt. Both use one shared symmetric scale with zero at gray, and the error maps (per-pixel RGB RMS) share one color range across all three times. The error is concentrated on edges, whiskers, and fur, and it shrinks as t increases, since there is less missing detail for the model to guess.

Part A 1.5: Iterative Denoising with Euler Sampling

euler_sample repeats xt+h = xt + h vθ(xt, t, c) with h = 0.02, recomputing the velocity each step and shortening the last step so it ends exactly at t = 1. Starting from the t = 0.5 input, this is 25 updates.

Euler recovers the whiskers and fur that the one-step estimate smooths over. Its MSE to the original (0.0114) is slightly higher than one step (0.0094), since the extra detail it adds is plausible but not exactly Oreo's. Both are far better than the blur (0.0205).

Part A 1.6: Generating Images from Noise

Running euler_sample from pure noise at t = 0 with "a high quality photo". Every sample is a portrait of a person, so the generic prompt favors people. Several are badly formed: seed 180 has no face and seed 183 is distorted.

Part A 2.1: Classifier-Free Guidance

cfg_velocity combines the conditional and empty-prompt predictions, v = vc + w(vc − vu), with w = 7. Using the same five noises, every face is now coherent and sharp. The tradeoff is variety: they are all front facing headshots against plain backgrounds.

Part A 3.1: Image-to-Image Editing

edit noises the image to the starting time, then runs Euler with CFG back to t = 1. At the lowest starting times almost nothing survives, and the model makes something unrelated (people, devices). Around t = 0.10 to 0.14 the layout and colors come back, and by 0.25 the result is close to the original.

Part A 3.2: Text-Guided Image Editing

Same procedure with a different prompt. Low starting times follow the prompt; high ones keep the original. The middle times mix the two, like the rocket that keeps the Campanile's framing and trees.

Part A 3.3: Visual Anagrams

At each step, visual_anagrams averages the CFG velocity for the first prompt with the CFG velocity for the second prompt on the image rotated 180°, rotated back.

Part A 3.4: Hybrid Images

make_hybrids uses the low frequencies of the first prompt's CFG velocity plus the high frequencies of the second's (Gaussian blur, kernel 265, σ = 16). Each is shown at full size and at 64 × 64 to simulate viewing from far away.

Part B 1.2: Noising Process

z = x + σε with one shared noise sample. Rows go from σ = 0 (top) to 0.2, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.8, 1.0 (bottom).

Part B 1.2.1: Training a Single-Step Denoiser

Unconditional UNet with D = 128, trained for 5 epochs at σ = 0.5 (batch size 256, Adam, lr = 1e-4), with fresh noise every batch.

Part B 1.2.2: Out-of-Distribution Testing

Rows alternate noisy and denoised for σ = 0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.8, 1.0. The model handles less noise than it was trained on, but degrades above σ = 0.5, with blotchy strokes at 0.8 (where the 4 becomes a 9) and broken digits at 1.0.

Part B 1.2.3: Denoising Pure Noise

Same setup, but the input is pure noise. Every output is the same blurry blob. Since the input carries no information about the digit, the output that minimizes MSE over all targets is their mean, so the model learns the average of the training digits. The loss flattens almost immediately and stays far above the denoiser's.

Part B 2.2: Training the Time-Conditioned UNet

The UNet now predicts the velocity x1 − x0, with t injected through two FCBlocks that scale the unflatten and first upsampling outputs. D = 64, batch size 64, Adam with lr = 1e-2, decayed exponentially by 10× over 10 epochs.

Part B 2.3: Sampling from the Time-Conditioned UNet

50 Euler steps from pure noise, same seed for every epoch. Epoch 1 gives stroke-like squiggles, and by epoch 10 most samples are legible digits, though some are still malformed.

Part B 2.5: Training the Class-Conditioned UNet

The class is a one-hot vector embedded by two more FCBlocks, so the modulation becomes c1 · unflatten + t1 and c2 · up1 + t2. The class is dropped 10% of the time so the model also learns the unconditional velocity. Same hyperparameters as 2.2.

Part B 2.6: Sampling from the Class-Conditioned UNet

300 Euler steps with CFG, v = vu + γ(vc − vu), γ = 5. Each column is one digit. The digits are already correct at epoch 1 and clean by epoch 5.

Removing the scheduler

Without the scheduler, a constant lr of 1e-2 never gets the small steps at the end that let training settle. I lowered the constant learning rate to 1e-3, about where the scheduled run ends. It skips the large loss spike at the start and reaches a similar final loss. The samples are about as good, with a few stray specks. The tradeoff is slower early progress in exchange for stable training without a schedule.
